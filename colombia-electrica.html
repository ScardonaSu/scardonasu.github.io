01 / CONTEXT & CONTRIBUTION

A connected journey.
A distinct role.

I designed the game’s interface in Figma and created screen-by-screen mockups for the development team.

Colombia Eléctrica — 2300 combines a space-themed PC game with a mobile augmented reality experience. This first case study focuses on the PC interface.

My work covered the sequence from the initial screens and player login to the spacecraft preview and weapon-selection interface. I organized layouts, information, and actions into mockups that developers could use for implementation.

The development team was responsible for building the game. My contribution to this project was UX/UI design and the Figma handoff.

The screenshots below show the implemented game. The original Figma file was lost; current design documentation is being recovered from these screens. New interaction proposals are identified separately.

03 / ACCESS

A focused entry point.

The login screen brings the game’s identity, a short instruction, and the credential fields into one central composition.

  • IdentityBranding and the space environment introduce the game.
  • HierarchyInstructions, email, and password follow a vertical order.
  • Primary actionINICIAR sits below the grouped credential fields.

04 / SYSTEM FEEDBACK

A transition into play.

The next screen retains the visual identity while showing connection information, a loading message, and actions to start or exit.

  • ContinuityThe logo and subtitle connect access and loading.
  • System feedbackStatus text communicates that the system is doing work.
  • Available actionsStart and exit controls appear below the message.
A review opportunity: “Cargando…”, “Connected anonymously…” and an available start action appear together. Their relationship should be clarified when revisiting the states; this capture alone does not establish whether the controls are active.

05 / SPACECRAFT PREPARATION

The vehicle takes center stage.

In the hangar, attention shifts from account access to the player’s spacecraft. The preview anchors the composition, with options to choose weapons or start a mission.

  • Visual focusThe spacecraft is the central element.
  • Vehicle informationA supporting panel identifies the selected vehicle.
  • Next stepsEquipment and mission actions frame the preparation stage.

06 / WEAPON SELECTION

Options, context,
and detail.

Weapon selection brings a catalogue, the spacecraft preview, and equipment information into a single view. Each area has a different role within the preparation task.

  • CatalogueThe left panel presents the available weapons.
  • Context & attributesThe center combines the vehicle with descriptions and statistics.
  • Categories & launchThe right side groups weapon categories and the mission action.

07 / IN-GAME INTERFACE

Information around
the action.

During combat, the interface shifts toward status and spatial information around the gameplay view. The implemented screen shows how the preparation journey leads into play.

  • Central actionThe reticle sits near the player’s main visual focus.
  • StatusCounters, time, and status bars occupy the upper areas.

09 / LOOKING AT THE WORK TODAY

A foundation to build on.

What the project demonstrates

My contribution connects screen sequences, visual composition, and mockups prepared for implementation. The implemented game provides context for how those interface tasks fit together.

What I would validate next

Clarify connection and loading states, distinguish inspected weapons from equipped weapons, review language consistency, and test the readability of the combat interface in motion.

No usability-test results or performance metrics are claimed in this case study. The opportunities above are a present-day review of the available screens.
